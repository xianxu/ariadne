Letter to Alamin: NexHealth’s AI Transformation

Date: 2026-04-13 From: Xian To: Alamin Uddin, Founder & CEO

Alamin,

I’ve been spending pretty much all my free time building with AI-native workflows — not just using AI tools, but restructuring how I work around them. The leverage is real, and it’s not what most people think it is. I want to make a case for why NexHealth needs to go through this transformation, and why it can’t happen the normal way, it takes a skunk works setup.

The Thesis

Here’s what changed: LLMs can now reliably execute multi-step cognitive tasks and processes. Not just generate text — THEY RUN THE LOOPS. They read context, make decisions, use tools, rinse and repeat. This isn’t the chatbot era anymore. This is a new operating system for knowledge work.

Every company will need to be restructured around this, eventually. The question isn’t whether — it’s when, and whether we’re ahead or behind when it happens.

For NexHealth specifically: we sit at the intersection of healthcare coordination, integrations, and workflows — all domains where AI loops can replace layers of manual process. The companies that figure out how to run on AI-native workflows will operate at dramatically higher output per person and ship higher quality product. I’ve seen this movie before at WhatsApp — a tiny team with the right architecture and organization outperforming orgs 10x their size; FB bought WhatsApp after their messenger simply couldn’t compete. The same dynamic is about to play out again, except the leverage isn’t Erlang this time. It’s AI.

Now, I want to be honest: AI-first development is not a solved problem. The raw capability is here — agents can write code, run tools, iterate on feedback loops. But how to sustain this in a specific domain, with production stakes, and keep it maintainable? Nobody has figured that out. How to build institutional knowledge in specs that agents can reliably use, or exactly where human checkpoints need to sit for correctness — all new territory.

And that’s precisely why the skunk works matters. It’s not just about deploying known techniques faster. It’s about developing the new software development discipline for our domain before competitors do. Whoever builds that playbook first has a compounding advantage that’s pretty hard to replicate.

Why Piecemeal Won’t Work

The natural instinct is to sprinkle AI across the org: add a copilot here, an AI feature there, let each team experiment. This feels safe and inclusive. It will fail.

AI transformation isn’t a feature. It’s a paradigm shift. It changes how work gets structured — not just how individual tasks get executed. When AI runs the process loops and humans steer, you need different everything. When your engine can move 10 times faster, all parts of your car need to be reinvented.

  • Hiring. You need people who can direct and evaluate AI output, not just produce output themselves. The skillset is closer to “technical director + architect” than “individual contributor who codes fast.” Pretty different from how we hire today.
  • Performance evaluation. Output-per-person changes meaning when one person with AI leverage produces what a team of five used to. How do you evaluate someone whose primary skill is steering AI to convergence on hard problems, and their ability to set up such environment where AI can converge faster? Our current perf system doesn’t even have a vocabulary for this.
  • Team structure and communication processes. In a world where a single developer beats a 10 person team in output, the amount and type of communication, how we track progress has to change, otherwise those processes become the bottleneck.
  • Process. Code review, sprint planning, roadmap estimation — all of these assume human-driven execution, at human speed. They become bottlenecks when the execution layer moves at AI speed.

You can’t change all of this piecemeal across an existing org, and through consensus. People have different capabilities, some will resist change, emotions and politics will ensue — not out of malice, just part of how people react to fundamental changes and how they are incentivized.

The Proposal: A Skunk Works

I’m proposing a small, founder-sponsored team with an explicit mandate:

Prove that a 3-person AI-native team can outperform a 15-person traditional team on a real NexHealth problem.

Concretely:

  1. Pick one highly complex domain. Synchronizer is a good area, particularly in the Cloud EHR where we are behind. Create a new Cloud EHR integration or two, AI first.

  2. Staff it small. 2-3 people max. People who are curious, adaptable, and comfortable with ambiguity. Not the most senior — but people who are AI native, iterate fast, while having the tool building mentality (as we will be building new generation tools along the way). I have Erik S. in mind for now, who’s the most advanced in AI coding after Jon. Dhruv is the other potential.

  3. Give it different rules. Different hiring bar (optimize for AI-leverage aptitude). Different evaluation. Different process (AI-native workflows, not sprint ceremonies designed for human-speed work). And empower them to use any tools they want to use. I meant ANY, they are free to invent any tool they need, not needing to run on existing infrastructure even. They may choose to, but not required to.

  4. Protect it. This is the critical part, and the part only you can do. This team should report directly to you — not through the existing engineering org. I’ll be blunt: the skills this requires are pretty fundamentally different from what the current eng leadership was hired for. The skills required — deep intuition for AI capabilities, willingness to throw out established process and way of work, comfort with radical ambiguity — are pretty fundamentally different from what we have today. A skunk works dies when it gets dragged into consensus-driven decision-making with people who haven’t seen the paradigm shift and don’t understand what’s possible. It needs air cover from the founder, and a direct reporting line to you, both for air cover and for your product insights.

  5. Timebox it. 90 days to show results. If a tiny team with AI leverage can demonstrably outperform on speed, quality, or both — that’s the proof point to expand. If it can’t, we’ve lost very little.

What I’m Asking

Three things:

  1. Your sponsorship to stand up this skunk works.
  2. A real problem domain to prove it on — something where success is measurable and meaningful to the business. I have some proposals in mind.
  3. Protection from premature scaling or dilution. Let it be small, weird, and fast until it proves itself.

I’ve been building and operating with these workflows daily. The leverage is real, but it requires a fundamentally different way of working — one that the current org isn’t designed to support. I’d like to show you what’s possible. Seeing it is more convincing than reading about it.

WHAT WOULD YOU DO IF YOU WERE NOT AFRAID?
