Good, I have enough context now. Let me think through this carefully.

**My thinking:** You're identifying a meta-problem: the agentic process is encoded implicitly across prompts (AGENTS.md, atlas/workflow/*.md), binaries, and their help text — and it drifts. You want AI to produce an explicit, living description of that process. The two approaches you listed have different tradeoffs. I should compare them on: accuracy, freshness, effort, and actionability. The history files (especially the `-plan.md` and `-close-review.md` variants) are particularly interesting signals.

---

Both approaches are valid but answer **different questions**:

### Approach 1: Read the repo (atlas, AGENTS.md, etc.)
This captures **intended** process — what the system was designed to do. The risk you correctly identified is **drift**: the docs may lag behind the actual binaries and prompts. This gives you a clean, structured picture but may be aspirational rather than real.

### Approach 2: Mine session history (`../pair/workshop/history`)
This captures **actual** process — what agents *actually end up doing* in practice. The history files show a clear pattern: `.md` (brief), `-plan.md` (agent's planning artifact), and `-close-review.md` (full session transcript including every tool call). The close-review files especially are a gold mine — they show exactly what sequence of actions the agent took, where it got stuck, what it retried.

---

### The real tension

[These two are complementary but the question is: **what do you actually want to produce?**]

- If the goal is a **reference document** for humans or future agents to follow, you want Approach 1 as the base, then diff it against Approach 2 to find gaps/drift.
- If the goal is **process optimization** (finding inefficiencies, loops, wasted steps), Approach 2 is far richer — the close-review files literally record every detour.

### A third angle worth considering

The `-plan.md` files are particularly interesting: they represent the agent's *own* model of the process before executing. Comparing plan vs. close-review reveals [where the agent's process model diverges from reality — those divergence points are exactly where documentation is missing or misleading.]

### Practical suggestion

Rather than picking one, a **two-pass approach** probably works best:

1. **Ground truth extraction** from a sample of close-review logs — use AI to extract the recurring action patterns (what commands are run, in what order, with what decision points). This produces a *descriptive* process map.
2. **Gap analysis** against atlas/workflow docs — what's documented but never done? What's done but never documented?

The output would be a living process document that's grounded in reality, not just intent. The key question is whether you want this to be a one-time snapshot or something that regenerates periodically.
